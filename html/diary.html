06/07/2026

This is the one time I will forgo my method of dating entries. I'm back from CSSRA natties (it was three days long)! I could not be more grateful for my rowing team. Yesterday, tattoos of a men's team rower's face were passed around in secret. It was his birthday today, and many people wore his face tattoo during their finals race.

It's honestly really tiring. An all out sprint for 2000 meters (2 kilometers) in a bid for the title of national champion (in canada). The first day is heats, which are qualifications for semifinals. Then semifinals hosted the next day qualify for finals. Finals is the day after that and by that point the exhaustion of regatta days has built up and everyone looks forward to going home. I raced two events, made semifinals for both, and made finals and won bronze for one. That isn't what matters though. The amount of fun that we were able to have as a team was the best part.

CSSRAs (Canadian Secondary School Rowing Association) nationals are held at the Royal Henley Race Course in St. Catherines, Ontario. The water there is this strange gatorade color that flows quickly past. For basically over half the race course, spectators can cheer on rowers in their exhausting pursuit of that shiny, golden trophy. Of course, (most) of the whole team would be cheering for their teammates.

After racing, I heard others cheering for my boat. Getting on land, other people were congratulating us, hugging us, that kind of stuff. As exhausted as I was then, I slowly realised that I didn't know how to express love or gratitude at all. It was this slow sense of dread. Oh. After an entire year's work, I still can't do these simple things that everyone else is doing around me, so naturally? I almost wanted to cry right there. I didn't know what to do. I felt so afraid of something. I still don't know what. But I couldn't go up to a teammate and say "OMG CONGRATS IM SO PROUD OF YOU!!" I knew that my voice would fail me.

So I didn't. And I watched everyone else congratulate each other around me. I wanted to burst into tears again. There are so many things that I wish I said to my teammates. I lost that chance. I just couldn't figure out how to copy the people who were always freely giving out love. I was afraid. So afterwards I sat on the bus listening to music and stewing in my own disappointments.

By the time we made a pitstop to the OnRoute I was delirious and emotionally volatile. Right before, the back of the bus had been in absolute chaos. Novice men were fighting each other with shoes and making a ton of noise. I literally could not fall asleep. We had like 30 minutes to buy stuff and eat so I bought Tim Hortons. I got a large chicken noodle soup and medium iced chai latte. For my first iced chai latte ever, it wasn't bad. I thought it would be more life-changing, though. I finished my food with 5 minutes to spare for the convenience store. They sold pokemon cards there. I bought a pack of Perfect Order with 0 minutes to spare.

That pack was around 11 non freedom dollars. I don't think I'll ever buy cards again. They're so fucking expensive. I'm sorry but I thought this was marketed towards children :( I got a regular Mega Zygarde EX (047/088) so if anyone wants that I'll trade for another card or something hit me up on discord. Then, I traded some nerds gummy clusters for a chocolate timbit with my teammate A.

To be honest I really just want to blend in with everybody else. I don't know how. I want to get along with everyone. Be friends with everyone. But I don't know how. There are bright and sunny people that I could try and be like. But I'm not them. As a middle child, I confess that I want to be someone's first choice. Not their only choice. Not their last choice.

It seems that everyone has gone from strangers to whispering secrets like best friends over the course of the season. I don't know how to connect with other people like that. It's like everyone hung out secretly and only left me out. Fundamentally, is there something missing? What's wrong? I read POPEYE (the magazine for city boys) online and I came across an article by Moka Kamishiraishi that went a little like:

"It's true that school, and classmates, are like people who happen to be on the same train. People who meet simply because they are living in the same era at the same pace. To put it nicely, it's like fate, but it's still quite difficult to truly connect with everyone. However, since we're all on the same train, we should be able to get along peacefully and harmoniously. From then on, I gradually stopped harboring any faint hopes, and I was able to enjoy myself, both alone and with everyone else."

After reading, I thought, she lowkey true though! In the end, I guess I want to be satisfied with getting along with everyone else. I'm not at that stage yet, but I hope to be there soon.

Mourning doves are these brownish birds that make a "coo" or "hoo" sound that often gets mistaken for the call of an owl. Even after all these years, my own instinct is still to think "ah, it's an owl" while looking outside. The call I hear most often is when males try to attract a mate. They're called mourning doves for their calls, which sound hauntingly sad- and therefore mournful. The first time I heard the term "mourning dove", I could only think of the morning, and how wonderful a dove would be to be named after the morning.

Mourning dove calls are low and relaxed. They take their time. To me, it's the most promiment call of all the birdsongs I hear in the spring and summer. I feel thankful for this bird, year after year without fail for giving me the same, recognizable birdsong. 5 months have passed. As of writing, the first 25 minutes of June has been graced with my presence. Happy Pride Month!

The cure to almost all of my problems in 3 words. "The simplest conclusion is usually the right one." I can't help but doubt it.
